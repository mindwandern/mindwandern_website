Together, they cover situations like:

  • Paying for a place to live
  • a rent rise that swallows a raise
  • a lease that runs longer than your contract
  • a room at home set aside for work

Mind Wandern starts a Path somewhere in that combined ground, and your picks along the way narrow it down. In the app you can set it more precisely still: place offerings on more topics, or a ward to keep a topic away.

Those are example Paths, shortened for this page.

The Path
the raise and the rent
Your new pay and the landlord’s letter arrived in the same week. You stand at the kitchen counter with both papers.

A question on the way

Once the rent is covered, what does your raise actually buy you?

Then the way forks. Tap the prompt that speaks to you more.

Pick a prompt above to see where it leads. Each one reaches a different question.

If you pick “When you read your new pay and your new rent together, what does the rent do to the raise?”

Next on the way

Which of the hours you work does the rent really pay for?

Further up this Path

How has your sense of home changed since the rent went up?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “Which part of your new pay have you already counted as spent?”

Next on the way

If your rent never changed again, what would a raise mean to you?

Further up this Path

When you picture telling someone the rent has gone up, whose voice do you hear first?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “Since the rent letter arrived, which month has felt most like a test?”

Next on the way

If your raise covered the rent with room to spare, what would you stop counting?

Further up this Path

After next month’s rent, what will you buy without adding it up first?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

Walk 2 · another day

When something stays unresolved, each walk takes a new way up.

A room can stay undecided for months. On another day, the same two topics start a different Path, and it reaches a different question.

The Path
the spare room
You and your partner stand in the spare room that was meant to hold your desk. Your job is two days a week now, and nobody has said what the room is for.

The way forks right away. Tap the prompt that speaks to you more.

Pick a prompt above to see where it leads. Each one reaches a different question.

If you pick “When you first pictured a desk in the spare room, what did that picture promise you?”

Next on the way

Where in your week are you still planning around an office that may never be used?

Further up this Path

What would you most like the spare room to hold for the two of you?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “Standing in the spare room now, what do you feel first about the job you planned for?”

Next on the way

When your hours were cut, what did you lose first: the income, or the role you had pictured?

Further up this Path

Since your hours were cut, what have you two said out loud about who the room is for?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “Whose plan for the spare room came first, yours or your partner’s?”

Next on the way

What would you let a room stop being, so that you two could still feel at home in it?

Further up this Path

Which of your shared plans for the years ahead feels like only yours?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.
