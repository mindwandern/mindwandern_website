Self-reflection questions about moving, staying put, and who a place makes you.

A new neighbor with a stroller asks if you have always lived here, and you start listing who has come and gone. Five weeks into a new town, the cashier still asks if you want a bag. Where home meets change

Home and place
Home, place, and where you belong
Self and change
Identity, change, starting out or starting over, and who you are becoming

Together, they cover situations like:

  • The one who stayed, and the one who just arrived
  • thirty years on the same street
  • the first winter in a new town
  • an accent that comes back on calls home

Mind Wandern starts a Path somewhere in that combined ground, and your picks along the way narrow it down. In the app you can set it more precisely still: place offerings on more topics, or a ward to keep a topic away.

The Path
the one who stayed
A young man with a stroller asks whether you have always lived on this street. You find yourself listing the neighbors who have come and gone since you moved in.

A question on the way

Which moment from years back first showed you that you were the one who stayed?

Then the way forks. Tap the prompt that speaks to you more.

Pick a prompt above to see where it leads. Each one reaches a different question.

If you pick “When someone asks how long you have lived here, what do you feel first?”

Next on the way

Which part of your street has changed alongside you, without anyone saying so?

Further up this Path

Which newer neighbor might one day be the one who remembers you?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “How have the years in one place changed you?”

Next on the way

When do you notice that you are the one who remembers how things used to be?

Further up this Path

If no one asked where you came from, which part of your street would you still choose?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “Who on your street would remember the way it looked before it changed?”

Next on the way

Which neighbor who arrived after you has ever asked about the years before?

Further up this Path

Whose footsteps on the stairs do you listen for without thinking?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

Walk 2 · another day

When something stays unresolved, each walk takes a new way up.

Settling into a new town takes a whole first winter. On another day, the same two topics start a different Path, and it reaches a different question.

The Path
five weeks in
You have been in the new town for five weeks. The cashier at the grocery store still asks if you want a bag, and you keep saying yes.

The way forks right away. Tap the prompt that speaks to you more.

Pick a prompt above to see where it leads. Each one reaches a different question.

If you pick “When the cashier in your new town asks if you want a bag, what do you say to yourself?”

Next on the way

What about yourself would you least want people in your new town to see?

Further up this Path

What is one thing about yourself you have decided to keep as it is?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “Which old habit from your last town do you still reach for each morning?”

Next on the way

Which word from your new town have you started using without noticing?

Further up this Path

Where in your days in a new town do you still wait for someone to recognize you?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “Where in your first weeks somewhere new have you caught yourself being someone you did not expect?”

Next on the way

Which things do you say yes to now that you once would have turned down?

Further up this Path

In a town where no one knows your name yet, who are you?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.
