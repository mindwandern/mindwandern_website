Where home meets meaning

Home and place
Home, place, and where you belong
Meaning
Purpose, faith, and the questions you have no answers to

Together, they cover situations like:

  • Rituals that make a house a home
  • words of thanks at a table about to be emptied
  • an evening pause where a prayer used to be
  • a first Sunday in a new city

Mind Wandern starts a Path somewhere in that combined ground, and your picks along the way narrow it down. In the app you can set it more precisely still: place offerings on more topics, or a ward to keep a topic away.

Those are example Paths, shortened for this page.

The Path
the bottom stair
Each night you stop on the bottom stair, where you used to kneel to pray. The quiet there has no words in it now.

A question on the way

Which evening pause have you kept on purpose, even after the prayers stopped?

Then the way forks. Tap the prompt that speaks to you more.

Pick a prompt above to see where it leads. Each one reaches a different question.

If you pick “When did your nightly pause first start to feel different?”

Next on the way

What did you once say in your evening pause that you can no longer say?

Further up this Path

Where in your daily life do you most want to be heard, even without an answer?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “What do you still hold onto in your evening quiet?”

Next on the way

Which old word would you still say out loud, if no one would ever answer?

Further up this Path

If nobody ever knew about your evening pause, would you still take it?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “Who, if anyone, knows you still stop at the same hour each night?”

Next on the way

Now that the nightly prayer is gone, what does the pause mean to you?

Further up this Path

When you stop each night, what do you still find yourself asking?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

Walk 2 · another day

When something stays unresolved, each walk takes a new way up.

Old rituals have to find a place in a new house, too. On another day, the same two topics start a different Path, and it reaches a different question.

The Path
words of thanks
You sit at a table that is about to be emptied. Someone asks if the old words of thanks will be said again in the new house, where the chairs are still wrapped in paper.

The way forks right away. Tap the prompt that speaks to you more.

Pick a prompt above to see where it leads. Each one reaches a different question.

If you pick “What about the old words of thanks feels most like yours?”

Next on the way

Of everything you will carry out of the old house, what would you refuse to leave behind?

Further up this Path

Where in your days does saying thanks together already happen, without anyone planning it?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “Who used to sit at your table when the words of thanks were said?”

Next on the way

Who from your old family table do you miss hearing say thank you most?

Further up this Path

What would you still want the people at your old family table to know you were thankful for?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “What would it be like to say the old words of thanks where no one knows them?”

Next on the way

Which part of the old words of thanks would you want to keep, even at a different table?

Further up this Path

Which word of thanks would you want a child at your new table to learn by heart?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.
