People close to you + The past

Self-reflection questions about memory and the people you share a past with.

Your brother swears there was never any cinnamon. An old friend laughs in a photo from the summer you were twelve. Start where People close to you meets The past, and pick the prompt that speaks to you more.

Where the past meets the people close to you

People close to you
Family, friends, coworkers, and others in your life
The past
Memory, loss, and the time before now

Together, they cover situations like:

  • Shared memories that no longer match
  • a family recipe nobody wrote down
  • a childhood friend you lost touch with
  • a parent’s habits you find in yourself

Mind Wandern starts a Path somewhere in that combined ground, and your picks along the way narrow it down. In the app you can set it more precisely still: place offerings on more topics, or a ward to keep a topic away.

Walk 1

Now find your question.

Follow one Path a few steps: take the fork that speaks to you more, and see the question it leads to.

Those are example Paths, shortened for this page.

The Path
the recipe nobody wrote down
You are stirring the stew your mother used to make. On the phone, your brother insists she never added cinnamon.

A question on the way

Which of a parent’s habits do you catch yourself repeating without deciding to?

Then the way forks. Tap the prompt that speaks to you more.

Pick a prompt above to see where it leads. Each one reaches a different question.

If you pick “Which moment of your mother’s cooking do you most want back?”

Next on the way

Where do you and your brother remember it differently: the amount, the order, or one step?

Further up this Path

Who in your family would you most want to hear say, “Yes, that’s how it was done”?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “When you stir the pot, whose hands do you feel guiding yours?”

Next on the way

At the step where you disagree, which part are you most sure of?

Further up this Path

When you remember a parent differently from your sibling, what does your version leave out on purpose?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “What did you never think to ask your mother while she was still making it?”

Next on the way

What did she do with her hands that you never once thought to watch?

Further up this Path

When your family argues over how something used to be done, what do you want to keep?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

Walk 2 · another day

When something stays unresolved, each walk takes a new way up.

Old friendships surface without warning. On another day, the same two topics start a different Path, and it reaches a different question.

The Path
the summer you were twelve
Someone tags you in a photo from the summer you were twelve. The friend beside you is a stranger now, but you would still know their laugh.

The way forks right away. Tap the prompt that speaks to you more.

Pick a prompt above to see where it leads. Each one reaches a different question.

If you pick “When you first saw the photo, what did you feel toward them?”

Next on the way

Which thing you once shared do you still do without thinking?

Further up this Path

Once the calls and visits stopped, where did the old closeness go?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “Which summer together does the photo bring back most?”

Next on the way

Which ordinary afternoon with that friend would you go back to?

Further up this Path

If a childhood friend walked up to you today, what would you say first?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “What do you notice now that you could not see at twelve?”

Next on the way

If that friendship had ended for a clear reason, how would you feel about it now?

Further up this Path

Where in your life does a lost friendship still matter?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

Mind Wandern is a self-reflection app that finds the question worth asking yourself.

Pick the topics on your mind. When the way forks, pick the prompt that speaks to you more, until you reach the one question worth asking. There is nothing to write, so it stays private.
