Self-reflection questions about attention, time and the people you love.

A phone lights up mid-sentence. A friend’s message has waited since Tuesday. Attention is what the people close to you notice first. Start where People close to you meets Attention and time, and pick the prompt that speaks to you more.

Where attention meets the people close to you

People close to you
Family, friends, coworkers, and others in your life
Attention and time
Time, attention, distraction, and where your days go

Together, they cover situations like:

  • Giving someone your full attention
  • the phone at the dinner table
  • a friend’s message you have not answered
  • the evening you planned with someone

Mind Wandern starts a Path somewhere in that combined ground, and your picks along the way narrow it down. In the app you can set it more precisely still: place offerings on more topics, or a ward to keep a topic away.

Walk 1

Now find your question.

Follow one Path a few steps: take the fork that speaks to you more, and see the question it leads to.

Those are example Paths, shortened for this page.

The Path
the phone at dinner
Your sister is telling you about her new job when your phone buzzes on the table. She pauses, waiting to see if you will look.

A question on the way

Who do you most want to be fully there for?

Then the way forks. Tap the prompt that speaks to you more.

Pick a prompt above to see where it leads. Each one reaches a different question.

If you pick “When your phone lights up mid-conversation, what do you feel pulled toward?”

Next on the way

What does the screen offer you that the person in front of you cannot?

Further up this Path

Whose face did you last look past to check a message that could have waited?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “Who has waited longest for your full attention?”

Next on the way

When you look up from your phone, what do they see on your face?

Further up this Path

Who in your life has learned to wait for you to finish looking?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “How often does a shared meal end with everyone still at the table?”

Next on the way

When someone you love is talking, what do you most often reach for?

Further up this Path

How do you split your day between the people you love and everything else?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

Walk 2 · another day

When something stays unresolved, each walk takes a new way up.

An unanswered message gets heavier each day. On another day, the same two topics start a different Path, and it reaches a different question.

The Path
the message since Tuesday
A friend’s message has been open on your screen since Tuesday. You have read it twice and not replied.

The way forks right away. Tap the prompt that speaks to you more.

Pick a prompt above to see where it leads. Each one reaches a different question.

If you pick “What stops you from replying to a message you have already read twice?”

Next on the way

What does the reply ask of you that reading it did not?

Further up this Path

What would it take to let a friend know they are still on your mind?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “What do you do with a friend’s message while it waits?”

Next on the way

Is it tiredness that keeps it unanswered, or the wish to get the words right?

Further up this Path

Which small gap in your day goes to the screen before it goes to someone you love?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “How would you answer your friend, in words that feel like yours?”

Next on the way

What do you want your friend to hear in your reply, however short?

Further up this Path

What is the first true sentence you would write back to a friend you have kept waiting?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.
