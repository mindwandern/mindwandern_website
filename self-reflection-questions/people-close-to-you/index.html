How it works Topics

Self-reflection questions about the people close to you, and finding the one question worth asking yourself.

People close to you is too much ground for one good question: a partner, a parent, an old friend, the coworker you see more than your family. So a list of questions about all of them stays general. Add a second topic, and combined they get you closer to the question you were looking for, even if you did not know it yet.

Pick two topics

People close to you
Family, friends, coworkers, and others in your life
Attention and time
Time, attention, distraction, and where your days go

Together, they hold situations like:

  • Giving someone your full attention
  • the phone at the dinner table
  • a friend’s message you have not answered
  • the evening you planned with someone

In Mind Wandern, a Path is a short series of self-reflection questions that starts somewhere in that combined ground.

In the app you can add more topics, or keep one away. For example:

  • + Work and money → find situations where work takes time away from the people you love
  • − Work and money → keep work away, and focus the situations on the time you do have together

  • Why not just a list of questions about relationships?

    A list might ask

    What do you value most in a friendship? How do you show the people you love that you care?

    Lists like that are written for anyone, about everyone. They are a fine start, and if a list and a notebook are all you need, that is a good way to reflect.

    But when someone close is on your mind, something else is usually tied up in it too: money, a move, the hours, a loss. Starting there gets you to a question you can actually answer.

The whole Path, with every topic, in the app

Mind Wandern is a self-reflection app that finds the question worth asking yourself.

Pick the topics on your mind. When the way forks, pick the prompt that speaks to you more, until you reach the one question worth asking. There is nothing to write, so it stays private.
