Community + People close to you

Self-reflection questions about showing up for neighbors and the people you half know.

A warm dish at the door of a neighbor who has just lost someone. A parent you know by her child’s name, asking if you can take her son home on Thursday. Small asks from half-known people can weigh more than they look. Start where Community meets People close to you, and pick the prompt that speaks to you more.

Where community meets the people close to you

Community
Neighbors, shared spaces, and the people you almost know
People close to you
Family, friends, coworkers, and others in your life

Together, they cover situations like:

  • Showing up for people you half know
  • bringing food to a neighbor after a death
  • a favor asked at school pickup
  • when a kindness starts to feel like a debt

Mind Wandern starts a Path somewhere in that combined ground, and your picks along the way narrow it down. In the app you can set it more precisely still: place offerings on more topics, or a ward to keep a topic away.

Those are example Paths, shortened for this page.

The Path
the dish at the door
The foil on the dish is still hot under your hands. Across the street, a door opens a few inches, and a neighbor who has just lost someone tries to thank you.

A question on the way

When you knock at a neighbor’s door with nothing ready to say, what do you hope your visit means?

Then the way forks. Tap the prompt that speaks to you more.

Pick a prompt above to see where it leads. Each one reaches a different question.

If you pick “What makes you hesitate before knocking at a neighbor’s door after a death?”

Next on the way

Which words have you already decided to leave unsaid at a grieving neighbor’s door?

Further up this Path

When a door opens only a few inches, how much of yourself do you let the other person see?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “Which part of carrying food to a grieving household feels heaviest to you?”

Next on the way

At a grieving neighbor’s door, do you want to be useful, or to be seen having come?

Further up this Path

Which neighbor’s kindness have you carried for a long time without thanking them?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “What did you last bring to someone who had lost a person?”

Next on the way

When you stood at a door without knowing what to say, what made you stay or leave?

Further up this Path

In a time of loss, which small gesture from someone has ever been enough for you?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

Walk 2 · another day

When something stays unresolved, each walk takes a new way up.

A yes at school pickup can turn into a habit. On another day, the same two topics start a different Path, and it reaches a different question.

The Path
the ride home on Thursday
A parent you know mostly by her child’s name asks if you could take her son home on Thursday. Your keys are already in your hand.

The way forks right away. Tap the prompt that speaks to you more.

Pick a prompt above to see where it leads. Each one reaches a different question.

If you pick “When someone you know mostly by their child’s name asks a favor, what do you feel pulled toward first?”

Next on the way

When did a kind favor first start to feel like a debt?

Further up this Path

Where does the line fall between a kindness you give freely and one you have agreed to keep giving?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “When a nod at school pickup turns into a request for your time, what changes for you?”

Next on the way

What would you have to put down this week to say yes?

Further up this Path

Which of the parents you only half know would you help without first counting the cost?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “Where in your week does a thin, half-known tie first ask you for something real?”

Next on the way

What do you hope a favor will leave between you and someone you barely know?

Further up this Path

When you next wait at school pickup, what do you want a nod to mean?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.
