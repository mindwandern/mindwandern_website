Community + Meaning

Self-reflection questions about faith, doubt, and the people who sing beside you.

Back in a pew after years away, sharing a hymn book with a woman whose name you have forgotten. Names read aloud at a service, one of them the bus driver from your corner. Belief is often held among people you barely know. Start where Community meets Meaning, and pick the prompt that speaks to you more.

Where community meets meaning

Community
Neighbors, shared spaces, and the people you almost know
Meaning
Purpose, faith, and the questions you have no answers to

Together, they cover situations like:

  • Faith, ritual and the people beside you
  • returning to a congregation after years away
  • names read aloud at a service
  • holding a stranger in your thoughts

Mind Wandern starts a Path somewhere in that combined ground, and your picks along the way narrow it down. In the app you can set it more precisely still: place offerings on more topics, or a ward to keep a topic away.

Those are example Paths, shortened for this page.

The Path
the shared hymn book
You are back in a pew after years away. The woman beside you, whose name you have forgotten, holds the hymn book open for both of you, and you sing the first line without knowing if you still believe it.

A question on the way

Where in your days do you still hold a belief you are unsure of?

Then the way forks. Tap the prompt that speaks to you more.

Pick a prompt above to see where it leads. Each one reaches a different question.

If you pick “When you sing words you are unsure of, what keeps you singing?”

Next on the way

Who in your life knows about the doubt you have about faith?

Further up this Path

The next time you are among people who believe what you no longer can, what will you do?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “How long has it been since you last sat among a congregation?”

Next on the way

When you sing beside people who believe more surely than you do, does your singing still count as yours?

Further up this Path

Who near you in a place of worship has ever asked you what you believe?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “What did walking back into a place of worship ask of you?”

Next on the way

If the words stopped being true for you, would you still stand with the people who keep singing them?

Further up this Path

Who from a congregation you left would you still want to sit beside?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

Walk 2 · another day

When something stays unresolved, each walk takes a new way up.

Some of the names you hold belong to people you know only by sight. On another day, the same two topics start a different Path, and it reaches a different question.

The Path
names read aloud
At the front, a reader lists names for prayer. Among them are the bus driver from your corner and the pharmacist’s husband.

The way forks right away. Tap the prompt that speaks to you more.

Pick a prompt above to see where it leads. Each one reaches a different question.

If you pick “When names are read aloud at a service, which one do you hold onto?”

Next on the way

When you hold a stranger’s name in your attention, what do you think that gives them?

Further up this Path

Which person do you wish well, without ever telling them?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “When a name read aloud belongs to someone you know only by sight, what do you do?”

Next on the way

Where in your days has a stranger’s name stayed with you longer than you expected?

Further up this Path

If your own name were read aloud, would you want someone you barely know to hold it in their thoughts?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “Who in your days would you want a stranger to keep in their thoughts?”

Next on the way

When you cannot help someone you know only by sight, what are you still doing for them?

Further up this Path

Which person you only half know would you be glad to hear was remembered today?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.
