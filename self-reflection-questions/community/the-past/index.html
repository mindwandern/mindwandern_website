Community + The past

Self-reflection questions about the neighbors who remember, and the ones who are gone.

A shopkeeper who has known your family for decades, saying you look like your mother did. A dark window next door, where a neighbor used to wave. A street keeps memories that no one writes down. Start where Community meets The past, and pick the prompt that speaks to you more.

Where community meets the past

Community
Neighbors, shared spaces, and the people you almost know
The past
Memory, loss, and the time before now

Together, they cover situations like:

  • The people a street remembers
  • being recognized as someone’s child
  • a neighbor’s house standing empty
  • the routines a street keeps after someone leaves

Mind Wandern starts a Path somewhere in that combined ground, and your picks along the way narrow it down. In the app you can set it more precisely still: place offerings on more topics, or a ward to keep a topic away.

Walk 1

Now find your question.

Follow one Path a few steps: take the fork that speaks to you more, and see the question it leads to.

Those are example Paths, shortened for this page.

The Path
you look like your mother
A shopkeeper who has known your family for decades leans over the counter and says you look like your mother did.

A question on the way

When did someone recognize you as your parents’ child, in a way you did not expect to matter?

Then the way forks. Tap the prompt that speaks to you more.

Pick a prompt above to see where it leads. Each one reaches a different question.

If you pick “When someone says you look like your mother, what do you feel first?”

Next on the way

Where in your days do you still answer to the name people once used for your family?

Further up this Path

As the people who knew your mother grow fewer, what part of her place in the neighborhood is yours to hold?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “When someone who knew your family speaks of your mother, what does it give you?”

Next on the way

Which of your habits or gestures came from your mother?

Further up this Path

Which small kindness from a stranger made you feel you still belong to the place you came from?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “Which faces on your old street still hold your mother?”

Next on the way

When someone says you have your mother’s face, what do you wish they had said instead?

Further up this Path

Which part of the life your mother built with others has been left in your hands?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

Walk 2 · another day

When something stays unresolved, each walk takes a new way up.

A gap on a street can stay open for months. On another day, the same two topics start a different Path, and it reaches a different question.

The Path
the dark window next door
On your way to the bins, you glance at the dark window next door. The neighbor who waved from it has been gone since spring.

The way forks right away. Tap the prompt that speaks to you more.

Pick a prompt above to see where it leads. Each one reaches a different question.

If you pick “Since a neighbor left, when do you notice the gap on your street most?”

Next on the way

Since a neighbor moved away, which everyday sound or sight do you catch yourself missing?

Further up this Path

Once the street has settled into its new shape, what will you still look for each time you head out?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “What did a neighbor living next door once make ordinary for you?”

Next on the way

Which small routine did a neighbor start that you still keep without thinking?

Further up this Path

Since a neighbor left, which person on your street have you started to rely on more?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “When you pass a neighbor’s dark window, what do you find yourself expecting?”

Next on the way

What has your street lost that no one has said out loud?

Further up this Path

Which neighbor would you most want to tell that the gap is still there?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.
