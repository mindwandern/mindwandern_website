Self-reflection questions about the small jobs that make a place yours.

The last stack of chairs in your arms while everyone laughs over coffee. A note by the elevator asking who has been watering the fern. Belonging often starts with a small job nobody assigned. Start where Community meets Home and place, and pick the prompt that speaks to you more.

Where community meets home

Community
Neighbors, shared spaces, and the people you almost know
Home and place
Home, place, and where you belong

Together, they cover situations like:

  • Small jobs in shared spaces
  • stacking chairs after an evening at the hall
  • a plant in the stairwell that someone keeps alive
  • being counted on by neighbors you barely know

Mind Wandern starts a Path somewhere in that combined ground, and your picks along the way narrow it down. In the app you can set it more precisely still: place offerings on more topics, or a ward to keep a topic away.

The Path
the last stack of chairs
Trivia night is over. Everyone is laughing about a question nobody got right, and you are still holding the last stack of chairs, unsure whether you are meant to stay for coffee.

A question on the way

Who in your town would notice if you did not come back next week?

Then the way forks. Tap the prompt that speaks to you more.

Pick a prompt above to see where it leads. Each one reaches a different question.

If you pick “What makes you want to stay, once the last chair is stacked?”

Next on the way

What do you tell yourself when you decide to stay, though nobody has asked you to?

Further up this Path

Which small task, done again next week, would make a place feel like yours?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “When the laughing starts, who in the room makes you feel you could join in?”

Next on the way

If nobody asked you to stay, how would you know whether you were welcome?

Further up this Path

Which evening with the people nearby do you already look forward to this month?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “Which part of clearing up feels most like yours now?”

Next on the way

When someone hands you a chair to stack, what does that small moment tell you about your place there?

Further up this Path

When you next see the people you cleared up with, what will you say to them first?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

Walk 2 · another day

When something stays unresolved, each walk takes a new way up.

A small job can turn into something people expect of you. On another day, the same two topics start a different Path, and it reaches a different question.

The Path
the stairwell fern
A note taped by the elevator asks who has been watering the fern. You know the handwriting from the parcel labels upstairs.

The way forks right away. Tap the prompt that speaks to you more.

Pick a prompt above to see where it leads. Each one reaches a different question.

If you pick “When did a small job in your building first fall to you?”

Next on the way

When something you do by choice becomes something people count on, what do you owe them?

Further up this Path

What would it be like to tell neighbors you are stepping back from something they expect of you?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “Which of the neighbors who rely on you do you know by name?”

Next on the way

When someone relies on you without really knowing you, what would you want to be to them?

Further up this Path

Which small promise to the people around you would you be sorry to see go unkept?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “When someone thanks you for a small favor, what do you do next?”

Next on the way

When the people who rely on you never learn your name, do you still feel you belong?

Further up this Path

When the people who depend on you pass by without a word, what do you take home from it?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

Mind Wandern is a self-reflection app that finds the question worth asking yourself.

Pick the topics on your mind. When the way forks, pick the prompt that speaks to you more, until you reach the one question worth asking. There is nothing to write, so it stays private.
