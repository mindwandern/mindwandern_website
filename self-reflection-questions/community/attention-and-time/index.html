Self-reflection questions about who you notice, and who you only scroll past.

A chair by the library window, empty for three Tuesdays. A lost cat in the street’s group chat, and you still reading every reply in bed. Attention is how a neighborhood knows who is missing. Start where Community meets Attention and time, and pick the prompt that speaks to you more.

Where community meets attention

Community
Neighbors, shared spaces, and the people you almost know
Attention and time
Time, attention, distraction, and where your days go

Together, they cover situations like:

  • Noticing the people around you
  • an empty seat at a weekly group
  • a street group chat that runs past midnight
  • the week you meant to check on someone

Mind Wandern starts a Path somewhere in that combined ground, and your picks along the way narrow it down. In the app you can set it more precisely still: place offerings on more topics, or a ward to keep a topic away.

The Path
the empty chair
The folding chair by the library window has been empty for three Tuesdays. You keep glancing at it during the reading group.

A question on the way

Which of the people you see each week would you notice first if they stopped coming?

Then the way forks. Tap the prompt that speaks to you more.

Walk 2 · another day

When something stays unresolved, each walk takes a new way up.

Some evenings the neighbors arrive by phone. On another day, the same two topics start a different Path, and it reaches a different question.

The Path
the street group chat
It is past eleven. The street’s group chat has lit up again with a lost cat, and you are still reading every message in bed.

The way forks right away. Tap the prompt that speaks to you more.

Pick a prompt above to see where it leads. Each one reaches a different question.

If you pick “What keeps you reading a group chat after the news is over?”

Next on the way

When other people’s news reaches you in bed, what from your own evening is still waiting?

Further up this Path

Which of the people in your street’s group chat would you recognize if you passed them outside?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “Who in your street’s group chat do you actually know?”

Next on the way

When your phone lights up after eleven, how much of your evening is still yours?

Further up this Path

When you put the phone down tonight, which message will you carry into tomorrow?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “What would your evening have looked like if your phone had stayed on the table?”

Next on the way

When you put the phone down late at night, what is the first thing you notice you missed?

Further up this Path

Before you put the phone down tonight, which part of your own day do you want to keep hold of?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.
