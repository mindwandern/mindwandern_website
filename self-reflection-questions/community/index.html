How it works Topics

Self-reflection questions about community, and finding the one question worth asking yourself.

Community is too much ground for one good question: the neighbor you nod to, the hall you help clear up, the bus stop regulars, the group chat for your street. So a list of questions about all of it stays general. Add a second topic, and combined they get you closer to the question you were looking for, even if you did not know it yet.

Together, they hold situations like:

  • Needing help, or offering it, next door
  • letting a neighbor carry something for you
  • a cough through the wall

In Mind Wandern, a Path is a short series of self-reflection questions that starts somewhere in that combined ground.

In the app you can add more topics, or keep one away. For example:

  • + Home and place → find situations in your own building: the stairs, the landing, the wall you share
  • − Home and place → keep home away, and focus the situations on the street, the bus stop, the waiting room

  • Why not just a list of questions about community?

    A list might ask

    Where do you feel you belong? How do you contribute to your community?

    Lists like that are written for anyone, about any neighborhood. They are a fine start, and if a list and a notebook are all you need, that is a good way to reflect.

    But when the people around you are on your mind, something else is usually tied up in it too: a shared cost, a favor, a change in town, a body that needs help. Starting there gets you to a question you can actually answer.

The whole Path, with every topic, in the app
