The body + The past

Self-reflection questions about the things you used to do, in the body you have now.

Your old duet partner wanting the fast middle section played exactly as you recorded it forty years ago, and your knuckles stiff since the fall. The bench on the loop where you sat together, and your legs asking for a rest you did not plan. Old routines come back, and your body meets them differently. Start where The body meets The past, and pick the prompt that speaks to you more.

Where the body meets the past

The body
Health, energy, appetite, and the body you live in
The past
Memory, loss, and the time before now

Together, they cover situations like:

  • Old routines in a body that has changed
  • a piece you once played faster
  • a walk you used to share
  • the pace you remember

Mind Wandern starts a Path somewhere in that combined ground, and your picks along the way narrow it down. In the app you can set it more precisely still: place offerings on more topics, or a ward to keep a topic away.

Those are example Paths, shortened for this page.

The Path
the old duet
Your old duet partner wants the fast middle section played exactly as you recorded it forty years ago. Your knuckles have been stiff since the fall.

A question on the way

Which of your playing years do you most wish someone could hear?

Then the way forks. Tap the prompt that speaks to you more.

Pick a prompt above to see where it leads. Each one reaches a different question.

If you pick “Who would notice if you played at your old tempo again?”

Next on the way

If your hands can no longer play the old tempo, would you still want to sit down and play together?

Further up this Path

Once the last note has faded, what would you want to still be true between you and your old partner?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “When did you last play the hardest part of a piece you love?”

Next on the way

If the old tempo is out of reach, what would you be willing to let people hear instead?

Further up this Path

Would you rather be remembered for the music as it was, or for playing it now with the hands you have?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “Which would you miss more: the old tempo, or playing with your old partner?”

Next on the way

What would you need to ask your old partner before you slow the hardest part down?

Further up this Path

If the old pace cannot come back, what would you still want your partner to hear from you?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

Walk 2 · another day

When something stays unresolved, each walk takes a new way up.

Some walks were always for two. On another day, the same two topics start a different Path, and it reaches a different question.

The Path
the bench on the loop
You reach the bench where you used to sit together. Your legs want a rest you did not plan, and you decide whether to sit or walk past it.

The way forks right away. Tap the prompt that speaks to you more.

Pick a prompt above to see where it leads. Each one reaches a different question.

If you pick “What makes an old loop still worth the effort for you now?”

Next on the way

When your legs ask for rest at the bench, what do you tell them?

Further up this Path

If the loop could no longer be walked the way it was, what would you still carry from the years you spent on it together?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “Which part of a walk you used to share do you miss most?”

Next on the way

When your legs tell you to slow down, what do you notice about how your body has changed since you walked it together?

Further up this Path

Where in your week does a slower pace feel like yours, and not something your legs force on you?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “What would turning back at the bench say about the walk you once shared?”

Next on the way

When you picture turning back, what would you be leaving unsaid?

Further up this Path

What would you want to carry into the next walk you take alone?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.
