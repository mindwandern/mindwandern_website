Home, place, and where you belong

Together, they cover situations like:

  • The news inside your four walls
  • a heating bill that closes rooms
  • an election argument at the kitchen sink
  • keeping a house warm on your own

Mind Wandern starts a Path somewhere in that combined ground, and your picks along the way narrow it down. In the app you can set it more precisely still: place offerings on more topics, or a ward to keep a topic away.

Walk 1

Now find your question.

Follow one Path a few steps: take the fork that speaks to you more, and see the question it leads to.

Those are example Paths, shortened for this page.

The Path
the heating bill
The envelope shows a bold number. The kitchen is the only room you keep warm now, and you have not decided which room to close next.

A question on the way

What would you go without so that someone you love could stay warm?

Then the way forks. Tap the prompt that speaks to you more.

Pick a prompt above to see where it leads. Each one reaches a different question.

If you pick “Which room in your home has gone cold first?”

Next on the way

When the cold reaches a room, what is the last thing you still do there?

Further up this Path

Where in the coming months will you still feel most at home?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “When you read a bill about next winter’s prices, what do you feel first?”

Next on the way

Where in the house do you sit when the cold is hardest to ignore?

Further up this Path

If the winter were harder than you planned for, who would you want beside you?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “What does keeping warm alone in a house built for more people involve?”

Next on the way

If the heating stayed off all winter, what would you have to admit to yourself about living alone?

Further up this Path

When the next bill arrives, who would you want sitting beside you as you open it?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

Walk 2 · another day

When something stays unresolved, each walk takes a new way up.

Some evenings the radio says what neither of you will. On another day, the same two topics start a different Path, and it reaches a different question.

The Path
the mug at the sink
Your partner is drying the same mug for the third time while the radio talks about the election. Neither of you has said whether tonight is a night for this.

The way forks right away. Tap the prompt that speaks to you more.

Pick a prompt above to see where it leads. Each one reaches a different question.

If you pick “When did you and your partner last talk about an election without it becoming a fight?”

Next on the way

Where in an evening does the question of who you voted for come up, even when neither of you means it to?

Further up this Path

What is one ordinary thing you could ask your partner tomorrow, unrelated to how they voted?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “On an ordinary evening, what happens when you keep your vote to yourself?”

Next on the way

When you and your partner go quiet over the dishes, what argument are you afraid of having?

Further up this Path

After the votes are counted, what will the two of you first say about something other than the result?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “Who do you wish would understand why this vote matters so much to you?”

Next on the way

What has your partner said about the election that you believed, even while you disagreed?

Further up this Path

When the dishes are done, what do you hope will be said the next time you stand side by side?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.
