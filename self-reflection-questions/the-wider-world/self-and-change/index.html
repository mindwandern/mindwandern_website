The wider world + Self and change

Self-reflection questions about political disagreement, and where your opinions come from.

A campaign sign on your father’s lawn again, and Sunday dinner coming. A firm view on a war and a vote, built from headlines, while your partner asks if you are coming to bed. Who you are shows in what you say, and in what you let pass. Start where The wider world meets Self and change, and pick the prompt that speaks to you more.

Where the world meets change

The wider world
News, your country, and the town you live in
Self and change
Identity, change, starting out or starting over, and who you are becoming

Together, they cover situations like:

  • Opinions, family and who you are becoming
  • politics at the family dinner table
  • a view you formed from headlines
  • staying quiet to keep the peace

Mind Wandern starts a Path somewhere in that combined ground, and your picks along the way narrow it down. In the app you can set it more precisely still: place offerings on more topics, or a ward to keep a topic away.

Walk 1

Now find your question.

Follow one Path a few steps: take the fork that speaks to you more, and see the question it leads to.

Those are example Paths, shortened for this page.

The Path
the sign on the lawn
Your father has put a campaign sign on his lawn again, and Sunday dinner is coming. The rent rules on the ballot will affect your own apartment either way.

A question on the way

When you say nothing at a family dinner, what are you keeping safe?

Then the way forks. Tap the prompt that speaks to you more.

Pick a prompt above to see where it leads. Each one reaches a different question.

If you pick “Which part of a parent’s campaign sign weighs on you most before you visit?”

Next on the way

If you spoke up at dinner and nothing changed, what would you still have said about yourself?

Further up this Path

Which of your views would you still want to say out loud a year from now?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “What would happen at the family table if you said nothing?”

Next on the way

Where in your life have you stayed silent to keep the peace?

Further up this Path

What do you want the people you love to still say to you after a political argument?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “How much of who you are is tied to what you will not let pass at the table?”

Next on the way

Of everything a parent has said to you about politics, which has stayed with you longest?

Further up this Path

Which political argument with someone you love followed you home this week?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

Walk 2 · another day

When something stays unresolved, each walk takes a new way up.

Some opinions arrive before the reading is done. On another day, the same two topics start a different Path, and it reaches a different question.

The Path
the headlines at bedtime
You have a firm view on a distant war and on tomorrow’s vote, though you have read only the headlines. Your partner has just asked whether you are coming to bed.

The way forks right away. Tap the prompt that speaks to you more.

Pick a prompt above to see where it leads. Each one reaches a different question.

If you pick “When did your view on a distant war first feel like your own?”

Next on the way

The last time you argued your view out loud, what did you want to protect?

Further up this Path

If no one ever saw you holding a view, how much of your certainty would still be yours?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “Who would you most want to hear your take on the next vote?”

Next on the way

Which of the things you feel sure about would still hold if you had to explain them in your own words?

Further up this Path

Once the vote is over, what will you go back and read properly?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “What is the last thing you read all the way to the end?”

Next on the way

When you speak with certainty about something you have only skimmed, what makes you sure?

Further up this Path

Which of your opinions could you explain to someone who has not read what you have read?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.
