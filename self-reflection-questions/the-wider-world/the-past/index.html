The wider world + The past

Self-reflection questions about the places your family left, and the news that brings them back.

A grandmother’s letters about a river that rose over her field, and the same water rising on your phone today. A father who turns the TV down when his old town is on the screen. The news can reopen a family’s past. Start where The wider world meets The past, and pick the prompt that speaks to you more.

Where the world meets the past

The wider world
News, your country, and the town you live in
The past
Memory, loss, and the time before now

Together, they cover situations like:

  • Old countries in today’s news
  • a family place you only know from letters
  • a parent who goes quiet when the news comes on
  • questions you never asked about leaving

Mind Wandern starts a Path somewhere in that combined ground, and your picks along the way narrow it down. In the app you can set it more precisely still: place offerings on more topics, or a ward to keep a topic away.

The Path
your grandmother’s letters
Your grandmother’s careful handwriting describes a river that rose over the field she grew up in. The news on your phone shows the same water rising today.

A question on the way

Which sentence from a grandparent’s letters would you most want to answer?

Then the way forks. Tap the prompt that speaks to you more.

Pick a prompt above to see where it leads. Each one reaches a different question.

If you pick “Which of your grandmother’s letters do you reread when news about her home arrives?”

Next on the way

When a new flood report reaches you, what do you want her letters to have said about that same ground?

Further up this Path

Who in your family would you share your grandmother’s letters with?

Walk 2 · another day

When something stays unresolved, each walk takes a new way up.

A parent’s silence can say more than the news. On another day, the same two topics start a different Path, and it reaches a different question.

The Path
the TV turned down
Your father turns the TV down when his old town appears on the screen. He asks you to read him a message from your cousin, who is still there with two suitcases.

The way forks right away. Tap the prompt that speaks to you more.

Pick a prompt above to see where it leads. Each one reaches a different question.

If you pick “When you read news from a parent’s old town aloud, what do you see in their face?”

Next on the way

Since the news came on each evening, what has your father left unsaid that you have started to hear?

Further up this Path

Whose voice, besides your father’s, would you want to hear about the town on the news?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “When a parent’s old town is on the screen, what do you want to ask them?”

Next on the way

What do you wish your father would tell you about the years before you were born?

Further up this Path

Which of your father’s silences have you never said aloud?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.

If you pick “Who in your life has talked to you about a place they could not go back to?”

Next on the way

When your father talks about the place he left, what does he leave out?

Further up this Path

Which of the things your father said about that place would you least want to lose?

Answer it in your head, or on paper if you like.
